Child Maltreatment Surveillance & Reporting Systems

Stewart-Tufescu et al. • 2022 • Journal of Interpersonal Violence

Why This Research Matters

This rapid scoping review examined how large population surveys collect child-maltreatment information from adults who know the child well. It found substantial variation in definitions, question wording, timeframes, and reporting methods.

Key Findings

  • Consistent definitions and standardized questions are necessary for reliable child-maltreatment surveillance.
  • Fragmented data collection limits comparison, trend analysis, and understanding of the true scope of maltreatment.
  • Better-quality reporting practices can strengthen prevention planning and system accountability.

Legislative Takeaways

  • Supports Wisconsin adopting consistent statewide definitions and reporting fields.
  • Supports collecting information in a format that can be compared across counties and over time.
  • Provides a research rationale for stronger surveillance and data-quality standards within a centralized reporting system.

Study Limitations

This study reviewed population-survey practices. It did not test a centralized child-protection hotline or prove that statewide centralization improves case outcomes.

Evidence Strength

Evidence fit: supportive for standardized data and surveillance; indirect for centralized statewide reporting.

Delayed Disclosure of Child Sexual Abuse

Latiff et al. • 2024 • Child Abuse & Neglect

Why This Research Matters

This systematic review synthesized research on factors that help or hinder disclosure of child sexual abuse. It shows that disclosure is often delayed and shaped by personal, relational, family, and abuse-related circumstances.

Key Findings

  • Disclosure may be delayed by fear, shame, avoidant coping, disability, family dynamics, and a close relationship with the perpetrator.
  • Disclosure is often a gradual process rather than a single event.
  • Systems that rely only on immediate childhood disclosure will miss important safety information.

Legislative Takeaways

  • Strongly supports an adult survivor reporting pathway for childhood abuse.
  • Supports treating delayed reports as potentially valuable child-safety information.
  • Supports statewide pattern tracking so adult reports can be connected to other concerns involving the same person or institution.

Study Limitations

The review did not directly evaluate a specific adult-reporting statute or measure whether such a law prevents future abuse.

Evidence Strength

Evidence fit: strong for delayed disclosure and adult survivor reporting pathways.

School-Based Prevention (Erin's Law)

Che Yusof et al. • 2022 • Frontiers in Public Health

Why This Research Matters

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated school-based child sexual abuse prevention programs and examined whether they improved children’s knowledge, protective skills, and attitudes.

Key Findings

  • School-based programs improved children’s knowledge about child sexual abuse.
  • Programs improved protective skills and prevention-related attitudes.
  • The findings support providing age-appropriate prevention education in schools.

Legislative Takeaways

  • Strongly supports Erin’s Law-style school-based prevention education.
  • Supports statewide minimum standards so access does not depend on a child’s school district.
  • Supports evidence-informed curricula that teach recognition, body safety, help-seeking, and disclosure skills.

Study Limitations

Improved knowledge and skills do not by themselves prove a reduction in abuse incidence. Program quality, implementation, and reinforcement matter.

Evidence Strength

Evidence fit: strong for Erin’s Law and school-based prevention education.

Organizational Child Protection Standards

Lu et al. • 2023 • Trauma, Violence, & Abuse

Why This Research Matters

This realist review examined how and why school-based child sexual abuse prevention programs work and identified implementation features associated with stronger outcomes.

Key Findings

  • Programs should match children’s cognitive and developmental levels.
  • Repeated exposure to key concepts and skills is important.
  • Interactive delivery, positive feedback, positive messaging, and train-the-trainer models can strengthen implementation.

Legislative Takeaways

  • Supports mandated, evidence-informed prevention training rather than one-time awareness materials.
  • Supports organizational standards requiring developmentally appropriate, repeated, and interactive prevention education.
  • Supports train-the-trainer structures for consistent implementation.

Study Limitations

The review focuses on school-based prevention programs and does not evaluate every element of broader organizational safeguards such as screening, codes of conduct, or background checks.

Evidence Strength

Evidence fit: strong for prevention-program standards and training; narrower than the full organizational reform proposal.

Mandatory Reporting Research

Glouchkow et al. • 2022 • Journal of Child & Adolescent Trauma

Why This Research Matters

This study used reportable child-maltreatment scenarios to examine teachers’ recognition, intended responses, and reporting decisions. It shows that a legal reporting duty does not automatically produce consistent action.

Key Findings

  • Educators’ responses varied across types of maltreatment.
  • Knowledge, recognition, confidence, and decision-making affect whether suspected maltreatment is reported appropriately.
  • Clear expectations and practical training are important components of an effective reporting system.

Legislative Takeaways

  • Supports mandated reporter training that teaches recognition and exactly how to make a report.
  • Supports clear, uniform reporting procedures and accountability for child-serving professionals.
  • Supports pairing any expansion of reporting duties with training, accessible reporting channels, and system feedback.

Study Limitations

This paper does not compare universal mandatory reporting states with profession-based reporting states and does not establish that universal mandatory reporting improves child outcomes.

Evidence Strength

Evidence fit: strong for reporting training and implementation; not direct proof for universal mandatory reporting.

Institutional Accountability & Transparency

Finch et al. • 2021 • Campbell Systematic Reviews

Why This Research Matters

This evidence and gap map examined interventions intended to prevent or respond to child maltreatment occurring within institutions and organized the evidence for policymakers and practitioners.

Key Findings

  • Institutional maltreatment requires organization-level and system-level responses.
  • The evidence base is uneven, with important gaps in rigorous evaluation.
  • Mapping interventions and outcomes is necessary to identify what is known and where stronger safeguards are needed.

Legislative Takeaways

  • Supports comprehensive standards for institutions and child-serving organizations.
  • Supports transparent monitoring, evaluation, and accountability rather than relying only on internal assurances.
  • Supports collecting comparable data on institutional incidents and responses so patterns and failures can be identified.

Study Limitations

An evidence and gap map organizes existing research; it does not prove that one specific accountability policy, penalty, NDA restriction, or reporting structure is effective.

Evidence Strength

Evidence fit: supportive for institutional safeguards, monitoring, and accountability; indirect for specific statutory mechanisms.
