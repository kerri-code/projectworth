Why Response Matters

Three research-backed facts that help explain why child-protection systems need strong documentation, response, and follow-through.

Delayed Disclosure Is Common

Children may delay disclosing sexual abuse, sometimes until adulthood.

Systematic review • Adult disclosure review

False Reports Are Uncommon

Intentionally false reports are estimated at approximately 2–8%.

A delayed, unsubstantiated, or unprosecuted report is not, by itself, evidence that it was false.

Current Policy Projects Changing Systems →
These are the policy areas Project Worth is currently researching, developing, and advancing to strengthen child protection in Wisconsin.
Scroll to view more →

Mandated Grooming Prevention Training

Require grooming prevention training for child-serving and oversight organizations so adults can recognize, interrupt, and report concerning behavior.

Universal Reporting & Accountability

Create a clear duty for all adults to report suspected child abuse, with stronger accountability for failure to act.

Centralized Reporting & Pattern Tracking

Create one statewide reporting system that connects reports, patterns, and concerns across jurisdictions.

Retroactive Pattern Review

Require known past concerns to be preserved and reviewed for patterns and ongoing safety risks.

Abuse Prevention Education for Children

Bring Erin’s Law prevention education to Wisconsin so children can recognize unsafe behavior, speak up, and get help.

Higher Standards for Child Serving Organizations

Use elements of Illinois’ Faith’s Law framework as a model for stronger adult conduct rules, employment-history review, and safeguards beyond background checks.

Survivor Access to Justice

Remove time limits for child abuse claims and restrict NDAs that can keep abuse hidden.

Wisconsin Leads on Child Protection

Move Wisconsin toward stronger child-protection standards already used in other states.

The Future We’re Fighting For

The Future We’re Fighting For

Project Worth is working to identify the strongest child protection policies already being used across the country, study where Wisconsin is falling behind, and help bring the best protections here.

The goal is simple: make Wisconsin the safest state for children in the nation by 2030. Then help make that protection the standard for every child in the United States.
