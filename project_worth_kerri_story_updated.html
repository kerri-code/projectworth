Kerri’s Story

KERRI’S STORY

My why. Our purpose.
★

This is not my whole story, but it is the piece that shaped my voice and commitment to change.

As a minor, I was abused and groomed by an authority figure in a Kenosha private school. I later learned the school had received reports of sexual misconduct involving me during my time as a student, but failed to make a mandated report and failed to notify my parents.

Years later, while earning my master’s in counseling and serving as a mandated reporter myself, I made the decision to report the abuse I experienced as a child. I believed speaking up would help protect other children.

I was wrong.

Instead of clarity, protection, or accountability, I came face to face with the gaps that still exist in reporting, response, and child protection. Because I was an adult at the time of reporting, CPS could not investigate, even though the individual remained in regular contact with minors.

I also saw some of the darkest behavior from leaders within the school and church where the abuse occurred. People and institutions that maintained a positive public image privately responded with silence, deflection, and self-protection when children deserved courage, honesty, and action.

Despite years of known concerns and predatory behavior, my 2020 police report resulted in no charges due to jurisdictional limits, and he was never formally asked to step down. He was praised publicly and featured in the school’s 2021 yearbook after I made the police report.

That reality led me to testify before our state senate and help create Wisconsin Act 200.

These were not isolated failures. I now know far more about the laws connected to my case than I ever intended to, and I have learned that Wisconsin has significantly fewer protections for child abuse than some neighboring and regional states. I want Wisconsin to lead in child protection legislation instead of playing catch-up.

My faith never wavered. My trust in systems meant to protect children did.

Today, I am a wife to my husband Zac, a mom to my cat Ellie, “Auntie Kerri,” and a professional counselor working primarily with children and families. I am also a foster mom, godmother, daughter, and friend.

This work is deeply personal, but it is bigger than my story.

It is for every child who deserved protection, every survivor who deserved to be heard, and every person who believes we can do better.

If you’ve been hurt, you’re not alone.

Project Worth is about turning silence into action. It is about advocating for stronger child protection systems, pushing for accountability, and building something that helps light the way forward.

For more on my advocacy and SB 333, please visit the advocacy section or review Wisconsin Act 200.

“Every time I thought about giving up, I thought about who this could help, and I knew it was worth it to keep going.

System Gaps in Practice

Illinois DCFS logged Kerri’s report of her own child abuse in 2026, which occurred primarily in Wisconsin between 2010–2012, while Kenosha CPS has no record. Kerri’s Illinois residency as a minor meant Wisconsin did not have sole jurisdiction, yet no reports were made to Illinois or federal authorities at the time. Illinois offers stronger criminal and civil protections for cases like Kerri’s, even when the abuse occurs out of state.

Wisconsin Child Protection Gaps

Wisconsin is one of the few states without a centralized reporting system and consistent pathways for adult survivors of childhood abuse. These gaps can allow patterns of risk to go untracked and unaddressed.

Project Worth exists to help close these gaps and build systems that act before harm continues.

  • No centralized mandated reporting system
  • Limited pathways for adult survivor reports
  • Inconsistent statute of limitations
  • Gaps in tracking patterns of concerning behavior

In this case, Illinois DCFS formally logged and tracked a child abuse report in 2026 that occurred primarily in Wisconsin between 2010–2012. Kenosha Child Protective Services has no record of the report.

Due to jurisdictional limitations tied to minor residency, Wisconsin did not have sole jurisdiction, yet no reports were made out of state or to federal authorities where stronger protections existed.

The report has since been submitted independently to Illinois and federal authorities, where centralized systems allow for documentation, tracking, and a more complete response.

Grooming Awareness

Grooming and the lack of a protective response can severely impact mental health. It is never “just words.” It is a pattern of predatory behavior involving manipulation, secrecy, boundary violations, and a betrayal of power and trust.

★ Research shows that psychological abuse can have long-term mental health effects similar to, and sometimes worse than, physical abuse.

Child sexual abuse is often committed by someone known and trusted by the child or family, which is one reason grooming can be so devastating and so easily minimized.

Source: U.S. Department of Justice, Office of Juvenile Justice and Delinquency Prevention (OJJDP)

What the Sunflower and Semicolon Represent

The sunflower symbolizes strength, resilience, light, and hope for those who have walked through darkness. It reflects healing, growth, and the ability to keep reaching toward light.

The semicolon ( ; ) is a recognized symbol of suicide awareness, survival, and the choice to continue. Just as a writer uses a semicolon when a sentence could have ended but did not, it represents the choice to keep going when a person could have ended their life.

Together, these symbols reflect survival, healing, and the truth that a person’s story is still being written.
